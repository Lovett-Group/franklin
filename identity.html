Graphic identity

Beyond the logotype itself, the POST identity has two parts.

Sticks

A system of lines used as graphic devices and as illustration.

Graphic Swap

A set of linear glyphs that swap out for the O in the POST logotype. The set is open-ended and is adapted to the message.

Sticks

  • Sticks are always the same thickness as each other.
  • They tend to be parallel — either all horizontal or all vertical.
  • Where sticks run perpendicular the ends do not join. They touch corners, leaving a small gap. That detail is what makes the system work for maps and diagrams.
  • At environmental scale the sticks become the linear structure of signage and wayfinding, powder coated in POST Blue.

Graphic Swap

  • A graphic that mimics the size of the logotype's O — a circle, for example — should be the height of the letter S.
  • A graphic that breaks out of the size of the original letters may grow beyond those boundaries.
  • Linear graphics are drawn at a stroke width of one third the stroke of the POST logotype as it appears.

The Graphic Swap is an open system that can accommodate an unlimited number of shapes, both abstract and literal, as long as they are linear.
